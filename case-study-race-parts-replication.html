Project Summary & Architectural Workflow

A motorsport race team commissioned the development of a custom, ground-up reverse engineering system specifically designed to replace scarce, race-damaged structural components. The primary objective was to bypass traditional supply chain constraints by establishing a localized, high-fidelity pipeline capable of capturing physical part geometry and translating it directly into track-ready components.

The Technical Pipeline: Rather than relying on manual measurements or standard third-party hardware combinations, this project engineered a fully integrated hardware and software solution. The system seamlessly bridged physical optical data collection, low-level data ingestion, parametric reconstruction, and industrial additive production.

Phase 1: Optical Metrology & Hardware Interface Engineering

To gather raw spatial data, a custom optical scanning apparatus was architected and calibrated from scratch. A proprietary low-level communication interface was written to manage data transfer and tight synchronization between the optical capture hardware and the host processing system.

Phase 2: Software Ingestion & Image Process Engineering

Raw data matrices from the optical apparatus were processed using custom image engineering routines. Noise filtration algorithms and feature-detection kernels cleaned the data blocks, smoothing out surface anomalies from damaged components while maintaining critical dimensional vectors.

Phase 3: Parametric Metrology & CAD Integration

The refined spatial vectors were imported directly into standard CAD parametric modeling environments as high-accuracy geometric constraints. From these verified references, precise structural boundaries were constructed, optimizing part geometry for localized load distributions and high-stress racing environments.

Phase 4: Additive Manufacturing & Deployment Execution

Bringing the digital assets back to physical reality required precise additive manufacturing tuning. Slicing profiles, toolpaths, and layer-bond densities were specifically engineered for high-performance polymers, producing multiple successful, structural replicants ready for performance validation.
