Project Summary & Architectural Workflow

A local waterfront community cafe commissioned a collection of custom, theme-driven tabletop installations: seven distinct sets of wildlife-inspired functional ashtrays, totaling 350 physical items. The primary goal was to take source photography from the natural world and convert it smoothly into functional structural geometries without losing organic complexity.

The Technical Pipeline: Instead of manually tracing or sculpting outlines inside design packages, actual wildlife photography inputs were fed directly into custom developed C++ matrix routines with OpenCV libraries to automate structural feature discovery.

Phase 1: Computer Vision Extraction

Using specialized matrix filters, image processing code extracted high-frequency contour coordinates from raw raster image blocks. Sobel and Canny convolution kernels mapped pixel change thresholds, outputting mathematics-driven contour vectors for edge verification.

Phase 2: Parametric Modeling & CAD Integration

The matrix boundaries generated in Phase 1 were imported directly into standard CAD parametric environments as reference paths. From those math vector definitions, precise boundaries were constructed to convert abstract digital shapes into durable, structurally sound 3D forms.

Phase 3: 3D Printing & Manufacturing Execution

Bringing digital vectors into physical reality required careful print optimization setup and material selection. Slicing configurations, build paths, and layer thicknesses were fine-tuned for high precision to ensure identical structural details across all 350 final units.
