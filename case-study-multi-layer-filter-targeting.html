Project Summary & Computer Vision Objective

This case study documents the engineering blueprint of a high-precision computer vision feature isolation and automated targeting engine. Designed for demanding industrial and analytical environments, this software pipeline scans high-resolution fields of view to identify, isolate, and precisely map coordinates for every target matching a specific profile. The explicit objective of this project was to construct a highly adaptable, multi-staged filtering architecture utilizing C++ and OpenCV libraries alongside proprietary algorithms that mitigate optical noise, leverage a Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) mathematical framework for blob selection, and calculate exact regional center of mass coordinates to overlay real-time targeting crosshairs.

Algorithmic Adaptability Blueprint: The core engine is engineered around a modular layer framework. By separating the initial feature narrowing from the secondary blob-detection mathematics, the pipeline parameters can be dynamically tuned or upgraded per application without restructuring the underlying coordinate-mapping codebase.

Technical Execution & Pipeline Architecture

The custom-built computer vision engine processes raw image arrays through a strict, deterministic multi-stage execution pipeline:

Multi-Layer Feature Isolation: Raw pixel data passes through sequential, adjustable filtering layers. The engine evaluates shape geometry, dimensional size boundaries, and exact hue ranges to instantly discard background noise and unrelated optical artifacts.

High-Throughput Adaptive Scaling: To maintain real-time performance on live video inputs, the engine features an optional adaptive scaling optimization flag. If a target profile reaches a designated dimensional threshold, the engine automatically decimates the input resolution—bypassing unnecessary, computationally expensive fine-scale operations. For seamless accuracy, the final coordinate targeting overlay (and user-facing filter maps) is automatically upscaled to match the original raw video dimensions.

Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) Mapping: Once the image is pruned and optimally scaled, a Laplacian of Gaussian algorithm is applied. This stage performs dual-purpose selection, accentuating structural blobs based on both physical scale and signal strength derived from the previous filtering layers.

Regional Maxima Extraction & Localization: The resulting LoG intensity map is scanned sequentially for localized peak intensities above a user-defined threshold. The engine then calculates the exact spatial center of mass for each validated maximum, programmatically mapping high-visibility targeting crosshairs to those exact coordinates within the field of view.

Cross-Industry Commercial Applications

By decoupling the filtering criteria from the targeting mathematics and offering high-speed throughput controls, this performance engine serves as a plug-and-play foundation across a massive array of commercial sectors:

Industrial Automation & Sorting: Rapidly identifying and isolating components on a high-speed conveyor belt based on physical dimensions and color profiles.

Defense & Robotic Guidance: Providing real-time coordinate streams for automated optical tracking systems or robotic arms requiring low-latency spatial orientation data.

Surveillance & Smart Infrastructure: Scanning security camera feeds or wide-angle surveillance video for specific visual triggers or anomalies in complex environments.

Biomedical Imagery: Automatically counting and pinpointing specific cellular structures or anomalies within dense, high-resolution microscopic fields of view.
